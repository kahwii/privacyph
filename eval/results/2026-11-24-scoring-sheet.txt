ANSWER:
  - When a personal data breach occurs, the personal information controller
  must notify both the National Privacy Commission and the affected data
  subjects within seventy-two (72) hours upon knowledge or reasonable belief
  of the breach [IRR-10173, RULE IX, Section 38].
  - This obligation applies because the breach involves at least one hundred
  (100) data subjects, and there is no delay allowed in the notification
  when the breach involves at least one hundred (100) data subjects or when
  the disclosure of sensitive personal information will harm or adversely
  affect the data subject [NPC-16-03, RULE V, SECTION 17].
  - The initial notification to the Commission and the data subjects may be
  based on available information within the 72-hour period [NPC-16-03, RULE
  V, SECTION 17; NPC-16-03, RULE V, SECTION 18].
  - A full report of the personal data breach must be submitted to the
  Commission within five (5) days, unless the personal information
  controller is granted additional time by the Commission to comply
  [NPC-16-03, RULE V, SECTION 17].
  - The notification to the Commission must include the nature of the
  breach, personal data possibly involved, and the measures taken to address
  the breach, among other required details [NPC-16-03, RULE V, SECTION 17].
  - The notification to the affected data subjects must include the nature
  of the breach, personal data possibly involved, measures taken to address
  the breach and reduce harm, contact details of the representative for
  additional information, and any assistance to be provided [NPC-16-03, RULE
  V, SECTION 18].
  - The personal information controller must ensure it receives confirmation
  of receipt from the Commission for the notification to be deemed filed
  [NPC-16-03, RULE V, SECTION 17].
  - For anything consequential regarding your specific case, please confirm
  with your DPO or legal counsel.

[8]  consent  (en)   confidence 0.719
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: Is consent we collected five years ago still good?

expected : NPC-2023-04
retrieved: NPC-2023-04 IRR-10173
retrieval_hit=true  top1=true  multihop=

ANSWER:
  Generally, consent remains valid as long as the information communicated
  in relation to the scope, purpose, nature, and extent of the processing
  remains and still holds true [NPC-2023-04, SECTION 21].

  However, consent is no longer valid under the following conditions:
  - If the scope, purpose, nature, and extent of the processing involved
  changes or evolves considerably, in which case a Personal Information
  Controller (PIC) must obtain new consent [NPC-2023-04, SECTION 21(A)].
  - If the consent was clearly only intended to cover a certain period of
  time or a particular context, it will not be equivalent to an ongoing
  consent for all future processing [NPC-2023-04, SECTION 21(B)].
  - If the data subject enters into a contract and subsequently cancels,
  terminates, or unsubscribes from it, the consent given to process personal
  data for that purpose is also terminated, unless there is another lawful
  basis for processing under Sections 12 and 13 of the Data Privacy Act,
  except for direct marketing [NPC-2023-04, SECTION 21(E)].

  PICs must also determine whether it remains reasonable to treat the
  consent as an ongoing indication of the data subject's current choices
  based on the original context and relationship [NPC-2023-04, SECTION
  21(B)]. Furthermore, obtaining consent is not a one-time compliance, and
  PICs must offer mechanisms for data subjects to exercise ongoing
  preference and control [NPC-2023-04, SECTION 21(D)].

  This information is for general guidance. For anything consequential to
  your business, please confirm with your Data Protection Officer (DPO) or
  legal counsel.

Q: Do we have to tell the NPC anything about the systems we run?

expected : NPC-2022-04
retrieved: NPC-2022-04 NPC-2023-06 NPC-ADV-SCRAPING NPC-ADV-AI-2024 NPC-2024-02
retrieval_hit=true  top1=true  multihop=

ANSWER:
  A Personal Information Controller (PIC) or Personal Information Processor
  (PIP) is required to register its data processing systems with the
  National Privacy Commission (NPC) [NPC-2023-06, RULE I, SECTION 4].

  Registration is required if the PIC or PIP employs at least two hundred
  fifty (250) persons, or if the processing it carries out is likely to pose
  a risk to the rights and freedoms of data subjects, is not occasional, or
  includes sensitive personal information of at least one thousand (1,000)
  individuals [NPC-2022-04, Preamble (1)].

  Additionally, a PIC must notify the NPC regarding any automated decision-
  making operation or profiling when the operation becomes the sole basis
  for making decisions about a data subject and such decision would
  significantly affect the data subject [NPC-2022-04, Preamble (1)].

  For specific guidance on whether your organization meets these thresholds
  or how to proceed with registration, you should consult with your Data
  Protection Officer (DPO) or legal counsel. You may also visit the National
  Privacy Commission at privacy.gov.ph.

[14]  security  (en)   confidence 0.676

Q: What security measures are we actually required to have in place?

expected : NPC-2023-06
retrieved: IRR-10173 NPC-16-03 RA-10173
retrieval_hit=false  top1=false  multihop=

ANSWER:
  Personal information controllers and personal information processors must
  implement reasonable and appropriate organizational, physical, and
  technical security measures to protect personal data [IRR-10173, RULE VI,
  Section 25].

  The appropriate level of security is determined by taking into account the
  nature of the personal data or information to be protected, the risks
  posed by the processing, the size of the organization and complexity of
  its operations, current data privacy best practices, and the cost of
  security implementation [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 29; RA-10173, CHAPTER
  V, SEC. 20].

  ORGANIZATIONAL SECURITY MEASURES
  Where appropriate, these include:
  - Designating individuals to function as a data protection officer,
  compliance officer, or someone otherwise accountable for ensuring
  compliance with data privacy and security laws [IRR-10173, RULE VI,
  Section 26 (1)].
  - Implementing appropriate data protection policies that provide for
  organizational, physical, and technical security measures, incorporate
  privacy by design and default, and provide for regular review and updating
  [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 26 (1)].
  - Maintaining records of processing activities that describe the data
  processing system, data flows, purposes, categories of data subjects, and
  security measures [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 26 (1)].
  - Managing human resources by selecting and supervising employees with
  access to personal data, ensuring they maintain strict confidentiality
  even after leaving the organization, and providing capacity building,
  orientation, or training programs [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 26 (1)].

  TECHNICAL SECURITY MEASURES
  Where appropriate, these include:
  - A security policy with respect to the processing of personal data
  [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 28].
  - Safeguards to protect computer networks against accidental, unlawful, or
  unauthorized usage, interference, and electronic network unauthorized
  access [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 28].
  - The ability to ensure and maintain the confidentiality, integrity,
  availability, and resilience of processing systems and services
  [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 28].
  - Regular monitoring for security breaches, vulnerability assessments of
  computer networks, and preventive, corrective, and mitigating actions
  against security incidents [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 28; RA-10173,
  CHAPTER V, SEC. 20].
  - The ability to restore the availability and access to personal data in a
  timely manner after a physical or technical incident [IRR-10173, RULE VI,
  Section 28].
  - A process for regularly testing, assessing, and evaluating the
  effectiveness of security measures [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 28].
  - Encryption of personal data during storage and in transit,
  authentication processes, and other technical measures that control and
  limit access [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 28].
  - Ensuring third parties processing personal information on your behalf
  implement required security measures [RA-10173, CHAPTER V, SEC. 20].

  SECURITY INCIDENT MANAGEMENT POLICY
  Controllers and processors must also implement policies and procedures for
  managing security incidents and personal data breaches, which must ensure:
  - Creation of a data breach response team with clearly defined
  responsibilities [NPC-16-03, RULE II, SECTION 4].
  - Implementation of organizational, physical, and technical measures to
  prevent or minimize breaches and assure timely discovery [NPC-16-03, RULE
  II, SECTION 4].
  - Incident response procedures to contain incidents and restore system
  integrity [NPC-16-03, RULE II, SECTION 4].
  - Mitigation of possible harm and negative consequences to data subjects
  [NPC-16-03, RULE II, SECTION 4].
  - Compliance with breach notification rules [NPC-16-03, RULE II, SECTION
  4].

  Please confirm with your DPO or legal counsel for anything consequential
  regarding your organization's specific requirements.

  Yes, the Data Privacy Act can still apply to entities processing personal
  information outside of the Philippines, depending on specific conditions
  linking the processing or the entity to the Philippines [RA-10173, CHAPTER
  I, SEC. 6].

  Specifically, the law applies to acts done or practices engaged in outside
  the Philippines if:
  - The processing relates to personal information about a Philippine
  citizen or a resident [RA-10173, CHAPTER I, SEC. 6(a)].

  citizens or residents, such as when a contract is entered into in the

  or affiliate has access to personal information [RA-10173, CHAPTER I, SEC.

  Philippines or holding personal information that was collected by an
  entity in the Philippines [RA-10173, CHAPTER I, SEC. 6(c)].

  Additionally, personal data breach management rules apply to any natural
  or juridical person in the government or private sector processing
  personal data outside of the Philippines, subject to the Act and its
