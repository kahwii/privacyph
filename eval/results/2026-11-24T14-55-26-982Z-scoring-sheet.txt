Q: Is consent we collected five years ago still good?

expected : NPC-2023-04
retrieved: NPC-2023-04 IRR-10173

ANSWER:
  Generally, consent remains valid as long as the information communicated
  in relation to the scope, purpose, nature, and extent of the processing
  remains and still holds true [NPC-2023-04, SECTION 21]. However, consent
  is no longer valid under the following conditions: - If the scope,
  purpose, nature, and extent of the processing involved changes or
  evolves considerably, in which case a Personal Information Controller
  (PIC) must obtain new consent [NPC-2023-04, SECTION 21(A)]. - If the
  consent was clearly only intended to cover a certain period of time or a
  particular context, it will not be equivalent to an ongoing consent for
  all future processing [NPC-2023-04, SECTION 21(B)]. - If the data
  subject enters into a contract and subsequently cancels, terminates, or
  unsubscribes from it, the consent given to process personal data for
  that purpose is also terminated, unless there is another lawful basis
  for processing under Sections 12 and 13 of the Data Privacy Act, except
  for direct marketing [NPC-2023-04, SECTION 21(E)]. PICs must also
  determine whether it remains reasonable to treat the consent as an
  ongoing indication of the data subject's current choices based on the
  original context and relationship [NPC-2023-04, SECTION 21(B)].
  Furthermore, obtaining consent is not a one-time compliance, and PICs
  must offer mechanisms for data subjects to exercise ongoing preference
  and control [NPC-2023-04, SECTION 21(D)]. This information is for
  general guidance. For anything consequential to your business, please
  confirm with your Data Protection Officer (DPO) or legal counsel.

Q: What security measures are we actually required to have in place?

expected : NPC-2023-06
retrieved: IRR-10173 NPC-16-03 RA-10173

ANSWER:
  Personal information controllers and personal information processors
  must implement reasonable and appropriate organizational, physical, and
  technical security measures to protect personal data [IRR-10173, RULE
  VI, Section 25]. The appropriate level of security is determined by
  taking into account the nature of the personal data or information to be
  protected, the risks posed by the processing, the size of the
  organization and complexity of its operations, current data privacy best
  practices, and the cost of security implementation [IRR-10173, RULE VI,
  Section 29; RA-10173, CHAPTER V, SEC. 20]. ORGANIZATIONAL SECURITY
  MEASURES Where appropriate, these include: - Designating individuals to
  function as a data protection officer, compliance officer, or someone
  otherwise accountable for ensuring compliance with data privacy and
  security laws [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 26 (1)]. - Implementing
  appropriate data protection policies that provide for organizational,
  physical, and technical security measures, incorporate privacy by design
  and default, and provide for regular review and updating [IRR-10173,
  RULE VI, Section 26 (1)]. - Maintaining records of processing activities
  that describe the data processing system, data flows, purposes,
  categories of data subjects, and security measures [IRR-10173, RULE VI,
  Section 26 (1)]. - Managing human resources by selecting and supervising
  employees with access to personal data, ensuring they maintain strict
  confidentiality even after leaving the organization, and providing
  capacity building, orientation, or training programs [IRR-10173, RULE
  VI, Section 26 (1)]. TECHNICAL SECURITY MEASURES Where appropriate,
  these include: - A security policy with respect to the processing of
  personal data [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 28]. - Safeguards to protect
  computer networks against accidental, unlawful, or unauthorized usage,
  interference, and electronic network unauthorized access [IRR-10173,
  RULE VI, Section 28]. - The ability to ensure and maintain the
  confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing
  systems and services [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 28]. - Regular
  monitoring for security breaches, vulnerability assessments of computer
  networks, and preventive, corrective, and mitigating actions against
  security incidents [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 28; RA-10173, CHAPTER V,
  SEC. 20]. - The ability to restore the availability and access to
  personal data in a timely manner after a physical or technical incident
  [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 28]. - A process for regularly testing,
  assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of security measures
  [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 28]. - Encryption of personal data during
  storage and in transit, authentication processes, and other technical
  measures that control and limit access [IRR-10173, RULE VI, Section 28].
  - Ensuring third parties processing personal information on your behalf
  implement required security measures [RA-10173, CHAPTER V, SEC. 20].
  SECURITY INCIDENT MANAGEMENT POLICY Controllers and processors must also
  implement policies and procedures for managing security incidents and
  personal data breaches, which must ensure: - Creation of a data breach
  response team with clearly defined responsibilities [NPC-16-03, RULE II,
  SECTION 4]. - Implementation of organizational, physical, and technical
  measures to prevent or minimize breaches and assure timely discovery
  [NPC-16-03, RULE II, SECTION 4]. - Incident response procedures to
  contain incidents and restore system integrity [NPC-16-03, RULE II,
  SECTION 4]. - Mitigation of possible harm and negative consequences to
  data subjects [NPC-16-03, RULE II, SECTION 4]. - Compliance with breach
  notification rules [NPC-16-03, RULE II, SECTION 4]. Please confirm with
  your DPO or legal counsel for anything consequential regarding your
  organization's specific requirements.

  entity in the Philippines [RA-10173, CHAPTER I, SEC. 6(c)].
